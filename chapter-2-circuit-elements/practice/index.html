S2Reading a dependent source
A source drawn as a diamond containing an arrow is labelled $0.4v_x$, where $v_x$ is the voltage across a resistor elsewhere in the circuit. Which description is correct?

Answer

The arrow inside the diamond makes it a current source, and its value $i_s = 0.4v_x$ is a current set by a voltage. So it is a voltage-controlled current source, and the gain is in A/V (siemens).

S5Resistor ratings
A 1 kΩ resistor is rated ¼ W. What is the largest voltage you can put across it without exceeding its rating?

Answer

$P = V^2/R$, so $V^2 = PR = 0.25 \times 1000 = 250$ and $V = 15.8$ V (the current is then $I = 15.8$ mA). (a) multiplies $P$ by $R$ and stops there, without the square root.

S8KVL around a loopPredict first

Predict first: on a clockwise walk, which elements do you cross from + to −?

Then find $v_x$.

Walking clockwise you meet the + mark first on every element: 1 (left to right), 2 (top to bottom), 3 (right to left) and 4 (bottom to top). The sign of the value does not change the marks. Clockwise drops: $10 + v_x + 4 + (-6) = 0$, so $v_x = -8$ V. (b) reverses one sign.

The worked solution below finds $v_o$. It contains the kind of mistake AI chat tools often make with dependent sources.
  1. KVL clockwise around the left loop, drops positive: $-60 + 4i_\Delta + 12i_o = 0$.
  2. KCL at the top node, currents leaving: $-i_\Delta + i_o + 2i_\Delta = 0$, so $i_o = -i_\Delta$.
  3. Substitute: $-60 + 4i_\Delta - 12i_\Delta = 0$, so $i_\Delta = -7.5$ A.
  4. Then $i_o = 7.5$ A and $v_o = 12i_o = 90$ V.
  5. $v_o$ is positive, so the answer is correct.

b) Correct it: with the right signs, line 2 gives $i_o = k\,i_\Delta$. What is $k$?

c) Finish the solution: $v_o$

c) Check it with a power balance: total power delivered

d) Line 5 claims the answer is correct. Why is that not a check?

Answer in a sentence, then compare with the explanation.

a) Line 1 is right. Line 2 is wrong: the $2i_\Delta$ arrow points up, into the top node, so in a sum of currents leaving it counts as $-2i_\Delta$. b) The corrected line reads $-i_\Delta + i_o - 2i_\Delta = 0$, so $i_o = 3i_\Delta$. c) Then $-60 + 4i_\Delta + 36i_\Delta = 0$, so $i_\Delta = 1.5$ A, $i_o = 4.5$ A and $v_o = 12i_o = 54$ V. S14Work backwards
In the S9 circuit, replace the 9 A source with a source of unknown current $I$, arrow still up. What value of $I$ makes the current in the 60 V source zero?

Answer

With no current in the 60 V source, $i_o = 0$, so the 4 Ω resistor drops nothing and the 8 Ω resistor has the full 60 V across it. It carries $60/8 = 7.5$ A, and KCL at the top node says that current comes from the source: $I = 7.5$ A. (b) uses $60/4$; (c) uses $60/12$.

Follow-up: why do the headlights dim while the engine is cranking?

Answer in a sentence, then compare with the explanation.

At zero current there is no drop inside, so $v_s = 12.6$ V. Each 100 A takes 1 V off, so $R = (12.6 - 11.6)/100 = 0.01$ Ω. At 250 A: $v = 12.6 - 0.01 \times 250 = 10.1$ V. Follow-up: the lights dim because the terminal voltage drops across the internal resistance: while the starter draws its large current, everything connected to the battery, headlights included, sees only about 10 V.
S16Two heaters: series or parallel?Two-tierPredict first
Two identical heaters are each rated 1100 W at 220 V. You connect them in series across 220 V.

Predict first: more heat than one heater alone, or less?

Then find the total power.

Reason

Each heater has $R = 220^2/1100 = 44$ Ω. In series $R = 88$ Ω, $I = 220/88 = 2.5$ A and $P = 220 \times 2.5 = 550$ W in total (275 W each): half the heat of one heater alone. (a) is the parallel connection; (b) follows reason (iv), which forgets that halving the voltage also halves the current, so each heater gives a quarter of its rated power.
